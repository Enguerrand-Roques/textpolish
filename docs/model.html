Two steps

1

Benchmark: which model?

17 local models, six rounds, each correction scored against an expected answer. Gemma 4 E2B won: good at correcting and rewriting, about a second per sentence, and small enough for a 16 GB Mac. Benchmark report

2

Fine-tuning: teach it the job

The misses left were rarer mistakes (“si j'aurais”, “would of”, the subjunctive), and a longer prompt made things worse. So the model was trained on 5,000 examples: messages with mistakes and their correction, informal messages to leave alone, and rewrites so Pro and Custom stay as good. Three hours on the Mac; only 0.6 % of the model was adjusted. How it was done

Most new fixes swap one real word for another (“except” → “accept”), so TextPolish shows them with an orange “Check” and waits for your click. Without any click, 707 sentences come out right instead of 705.

How we know the gain is real

  • The test came first. A new test set and the rule to adopt the model (fewer wrong fixes, at least one point better, Pro and Custom unchanged) were published before any training data was written.
  • A control model. Google's original weights went through the exact same conversion without training: they scored like the old model, so the gain comes from the training, not the conversion.
  • No test sentence in the training data. Every example too close to a test sentence was removed (31 of them).
